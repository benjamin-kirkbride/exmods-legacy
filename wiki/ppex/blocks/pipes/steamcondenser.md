A T-shaped fitting: the stem is a steam port and the bar is a water line running straight through
it. It draws up to 30 L/s of steam from the stem, condenses it at the usual 16 L of steam to 1 L of
water, and adds that into the water crossing the bar, which it passes at up to 50 L/s. The fuller
of the two bar faces is the inlet and the other the outlet, and the inlet's own pressure carries on
downstream of it.

It is a connector, not a network node: the three runs that meet here, the steam stem and the two
halves of the water bar, stay three separate networks, and the condenser bridges them itself. Leave
either side unplumbed and it shows: steam with no water line vents as gas, and water with no
outlet line backs up and sprays.
