A directional overflow: it watches the network on its input face, marked by the copper-trimmed
ring, and once that side tops the gate pressure it spills everything above it into the output side.
Right-click with an empty hand to raise the gate in 0.25 atm steps, sneak and right-click to lower
it, from 0 up to the pipe's own rating, 5 atm iron and 10 steel. With nothing piped to the output
it vents to air at the ordinary leak rate instead.

It only flows downhill, from the higher side to the lower, so it is not a backflow preventer and an
overflow wired back into a line already at or above its source moves nothing. Do not set one
in-line between a boiler and its engine: a pressure valve is a network endpoint, so it would split
the run in two and then feed the engine's half until both sides match, which is the pressure it was
meant to stop. Hang it off a junction on the engine's own run instead, with the output side left to
vent.
